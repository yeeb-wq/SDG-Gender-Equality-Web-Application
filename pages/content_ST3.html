Forms of Gender-Based Discrimination in Education and Workplaces

Gender-based discrimination in education and workplaces remains a significant barrier to achieving gender equality. In educational settings, discriminatory practices such as early marriage, gender-based violence, and biased laws and policies continue to prevent millions of girls from enrolling and completing their education. School-related gender-based violence (SRGBV) violates children's fundamental human rights and is a form of gender discrimination, negatively affecting school performance and leading to higher dropout rates, especially when the school environment is perceived as unsafe for learners. In the workplace, women often encounter sexual violence and exploitation, an unequal division of unpaid care and domestic work, and discrimination in public office, all of which remain substantial obstacles to gender equality. Additionally, harassment, including sexual harassment, is prevalent and is defined as any improper and unwelcome conduct that might reasonably be expected or perceived to cause offense or humiliation to another person. ​ These forms of discrimination not only violate fundamental human rights but also impede social and economic development by limiting women's and girls' access to opportunities and resources.

Strategies for Promoting Gender-Inclusive Environments

Using gender-inclusive language means speaking and writing in a way that does not discriminate against a particular sex, social gender, or gender identity, and does not perpetuate gender stereotypes. It is also important to make gender visible when it is relevant for communication while avoiding making gender visible when it is not relevant. Implementing action plans on gender equality is crucial, as these plans set out measures such as including gender-smart goals or actions in staff members' work plans; organizing training such as sessions on implicit bias and gender stereotyping; coaching on leadership, communication, and negotiation skills for female staff; and discussions on women in leadership. Providing equal opportunities for leadership ensures that both women and men have equal chances to assume supervisory roles and participate in activities that are essential for career advancement. Additionally, raising awareness of gender issues through regular communication, such as internal newsletters from the Under-Secretary-General and communications to staff, as well as articles on the United Nations intranet, is key to fostering inclusivity. By implementing these strategies, organizations can create environments that respect and promote gender equality, aligning with the United Nations' guidelines and objectives.

The Role of Advocacy Groups and Movements in Gender Equality

A global movement is underway to create a more inclusive approach to energy access, with women and marginalized groups playing a central role—not as victims, but as agents and accelerators of change. This shift is reinforced by several international agreements that provide a framework for integrating gender considerations into energy policies. The Istanbul Programme of Action (2011-2020) prioritizes energy access, gender equality, and women's empowerment for Least Developed Countries (LDCs). Similarly, the Sustainable Energy for All (SEforALL) initiative and the 2016 Paris Agreement on climate change recognize the intersection between gender equality, climate action, and sustainable energy. Beyond the global level, regional policies are also driving gender-inclusive change. For instance, the ECOWAS Policy for Gender Mainstreaming in Energy Access, adopted in 2017, represents a commitment by 15 West African countries to address gender barriers in expanding energy access. These initiatives highlight a growing recognition that gender equality is fundamental to achieving sustainable energy solutions, ensuring that policies and interventions actively include and empower women as leaders and decision-makers in the energy sector.

Ways Individuals Can Support Gender Equality in Daily Life

Individuals can support gender equality in their daily lives by taking action in various ways. They can challenge discriminatory behaviors and advocate for equal treatment in their communities to help end all forms of discrimination against women and girls. Supporting victims, promoting awareness, and opposing violence in any form are essential steps in eliminating all forms of violence against women and girls. Additionally, community members can educate others about the harms of child, early, and forced marriage while supporting initiatives that combat these harmful practices. Recognizing and valuing unpaid care and domestic work is another crucial aspect, where individuals can promote shared domestic responsibilities and acknowledge the importance of unpaid labor. Ensuring women's full and effective participation in leadership roles is equally important, and individuals can contribute by supporting and encouraging women’s leadership in various sectors. By aligning personal actions with these targets, individuals can actively contribute to advancing gender equality in their everyday lives.
